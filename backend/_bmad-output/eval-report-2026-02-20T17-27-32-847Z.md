Information density is outstanding: unique_dimensions (0.95), actionability (0.80), and inference_potential (0.90) are all high because the behavioral reveal maps directly to coaching interventions around approach anxiety and social skills. Cognitive load is the lowest in the batch (recall_vs_analysis: 0.80, answer_effort: 1.0) because the vivid, concrete scenario triggers instant gut-reaction rather than analysis. Psychological safety is strong: defensiveness_trigger (0.85) is high due to the playful hypothetical framing, and the full spectrum of options from bold to avoidant signals all answers are acceptable. Even on the weaker emotional leverage axis, it scores relatively well on commitment_signal (0.60) by implicitly revealing willingness to endure social discomfort. The question's genius is that it feels like a fun conversation while extracting deep behavioral data.

**Missing Dimensions:** Vision of ideal partner — no question asks what qualities or values they seek in a long-term partner, Future relationship vision — no question captures what their ideal partnership looks like (identity-level aspiration), Attachment style / emotional needs — only indirectly touched via Q2's failure patterns, never directly explored, Deal-breakers and non-negotiables — critical for long-term partner matching, completely absent, Current dating methods/platforms — no question asks how they're currently trying to meet people, Past relationship successes — Q2 focuses only on failures; what worked well is unexplored, Support system and accountability — who in their life supports this goal, Self-awareness of own strengths as a partner — what they bring to a relationship
