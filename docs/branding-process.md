# Momentum Branding Process

This document lays out a practical process for designing the full brand for Momentum, with first-pass answers filled in so you can refine them later.

## Core principle

Start with strategy, not the logo. If the strategy is clear, the visuals will feel coherent. If the strategy is vague, the branding will feel random no matter how polished it looks.

## The process

1. Define the brand core
2. Define the brand voice
3. Build the visual direction
4. Turn it into a design system
5. Apply it to the product
6. Create a simple brand guide
7. Test and refine

---

## 1. Brand brief

Write a one-page brand brief before designing anything.

### What does the app help people become?

Momentum helps people become consistent, focused, self-trusting achievers who can make real progress on one meaningful goal without burning out.

### Who is it for exactly?

Momentum is for ambitious people who care deeply about achieving something meaningful, but feel overwhelmed by the gap between the big vision and daily execution.

Primary audience:

- Young professionals
- Founders and creators
- Students with serious goals
- Career switchers
- Self-improvement-oriented people who want structure, not just inspiration

### What emotional state should users feel before and after using it?

Before using Momentum:

- Overwhelmed
- Scattered
- Guilty about inconsistency
- Unsure what to do next
- Motivated, but stuck

After using Momentum:

- Clear
- Calm
- Directed
- Supported
- Capable
- Proud of steady progress

### What makes it different from other productivity or coaching apps?

Momentum is not just a task app and not just an AI chat app. It takes one important goal and turns it into a clear, personalized path made of milestones, weekly plans, and daily actions. The core difference is that it combines structure, accountability, and emotional support in one experience.

### What should the brand feel like in 3 to 5 words?

- Focused
- Human
- Calm
- Energizing
- Credible

### Brand brief summary

Momentum is an AI-powered coaching app that helps ambitious people make meaningful progress on one important goal. The brand should feel clear, supportive, premium, and emotionally intelligent.

---

## 2. Positioning

Write the positioning in four parts.

### Category

AI personal coaching and goal execution app.

### Audience

People with serious ambitions who want help turning big goals into consistent daily progress.

### Promise

Momentum helps users turn ambition into a clear path and stay consistent through structure, coaching, and daily follow-through.

### Difference

Unlike generic productivity apps that only organize tasks, or AI tools that only talk, Momentum guides users through an actual transformation path with personalized planning, support, and accountability.

### Positioning statement

Momentum is an AI coaching app for people pursuing one important goal, helping them turn ambition into a clear daily path through structure, support, and accountability.

---

## 3. Brand personality

Choose what the brand should be, and what it should never feel like.

### Traits to aim for

- Grounded
- Motivating
- Intelligent

### Traits to avoid

- Robotic
- Hypey
- Clinical

### Personality filter

Every design and copy decision should pass this test:

- Does this feel grounded rather than trendy?
- Does this motivate without sounding cheesy?
- Does this feel intelligent without becoming cold?

---

## 4. Verbal identity

Define how the brand sounds in writing.

### Voice principles

- Clear, not corporate
- Supportive, not cheesy
- Confident, not pushy
- Smart, not academic
- Calm, not passive

### Brand voice description

Momentum should sound like a thoughtful coach who sees the bigger picture, gives practical guidance, and makes progress feel possible. It should never sound like a motivational speaker, a therapist, or a machine.

### Tagline directions

- Make real progress.
- One goal. Clear path.
- Turn ambition into momentum.
- Progress you can feel.
- The path from goal to done.

### Sample copy

#### App Store subtitle

Turn one meaningful goal into a clear daily path.

#### Onboarding headline

Let us turn your goal into a plan you can actually follow.

#### Push notification

Your next step is ready. Keep the momentum going.

#### Empty state

Big progress starts small. Your first step will appear here.

#### Paywall headline

Get your full coaching system, not just another to-do list.

## 5. Moodboard and references

Before making visuals, collect references in three separate moodboards.

### A. Typography

Look for type that feels:

- Modern but not sterile
- Premium but not luxury-fashion
- Warm but still precise

Suggested direction:

- Display font with character for headlines
- Clean, highly readable UI font for product text

### B. Color and atmosphere

Look for references that feel:

- Calm but energetic
- Focused but not dull
- Premium but not elitist

Suggested direction:

- Warm neutrals
- A rich primary color
- One confident accent color
- Soft depth through gradients, texture, or layered surfaces

### C. UI and composition

Look for interfaces that feel:

- Structured
- Intentional
- Spacious
- Directional

Avoid generic startup dashboards, overuse of glassmorphism, and loud AI neon aesthetics.

### Where to look

- Figma Community
- Pinterest
- Are.na
- Editorial design references
- Sports performance brands
- Wellness brands
- Book covers
- High-end productivity tools

### Important rule

Do not start by collecting logo inspiration. Start with atmosphere, typography, layout, and feeling.

---

## 6. Visual concept

Write a one-sentence visual thesis before designing.

### Recommended concept for Momentum

Momentum should feel like a modern coaching brand that combines calm clarity with forward motion.

This means:

- Not chaotic
- Not hyper-techy
- Not playful in a childish way
- Not overly minimal to the point of feeling empty

The visual system should communicate progress, trust, and emotional steadiness.

---

## 7. Core visual system

Build the visual identity in this order.

### A. Color palette

Start small.

Recommended structure:

- 1 primary color
- 1 secondary color
- 2 to 3 neutrals
- 1 accent color maximum

Suggested first-pass direction for Momentum:

- Primary: deep moss or forest green
- Secondary: warm sand or stone
- Neutrals: off-white, charcoal, muted gray
- Accent: muted amber or copper

Why this works:

- Green suggests growth, steadiness, and progress
- Warm neutrals keep it human and premium
- Amber adds energy without becoming loud

Avoid:

- Generic blue SaaS palette
- Purple AI gradients by default
- Overly bright startup colors

### B. Typography

Use one display font and one UI font.

Suggested style direction:

- Display: something expressive, confident, slightly editorial
- UI: something highly readable and clean

What to look for:

- Headline font should have personality
- UI font should disappear into usability
- Together they should feel premium and modern

### C. Shape language

Define how the interface feels physically.

Recommended direction:

- Rounded corners, but not too playful
- Clean card surfaces
- Strong spacing rhythm
- Minimal border use
- Soft depth instead of heavy shadows

### D. Imagery and illustration

If you use imagery, it should reinforce the emotional promise.

Possible directions:

- Abstract shapes that suggest movement and direction
- Editorial-style imagery about focus, discipline, and growth
- Diagrams or path-based motifs that imply progression

Avoid:

- Stock-photo team meetings
- Futuristic robots or AI brains
- Overly cartoonish illustrations

### E. Motion

Use a few meaningful motions rather than many generic ones.

Recommended motion language:

- Gentle reveals
- Step-by-step transitions
- Progress-based movement
- Subtle state changes that reward action

The motion should feel directional and reassuring, not flashy.

## 8. Logo process

Do the logo after the strategy and visual direction are clear.

### Best approach

1. Start with a wordmark
2. Explore custom letter tweaks
3. Only make a symbol if the wordmark alone is not enough
4. Test it at app icon size and product-header size

### Direction for Momentum

The logo should feel steady, modern, and directional. It should not try too hard to look futuristic. A subtle sense of movement or forward pull is enough.

### Important note

A strong wordmark plus a good app icon is often better than forcing a complicated symbol.

---

## 9. Apply the brand to the product

Branding becomes real when it works inside the app.

Design these five screens first:

1. Splash or launch screen
2. Onboarding screen
3. Home dashboard
4. Daily task or check-in screen
5. Progress or roadmap screen

### What to evaluate

- Does the product feel like the same brand across all screens?
- Does the brand support usability?
- Does the emotional tone match the promise?
- Does it still feel strong on mobile?

If the branding only works in a logo presentation and not in the real app, it is not finished.

---

## 10. Build a mini design system

Turn the branding into reusable rules.

### Define these tokens and components

- Color tokens
- Text styles
- Button styles
- Card styles
- Input styles
- Icon style
- Radius values
- Shadow rules
- Border rules
- Motion rules

### Why this matters

This is what keeps the brand consistent across onboarding, roadmap, tasks, growth screens, marketing pages, and future features.

---

## 11. Create a brand guide

Document the brand in one place.

### Include

- Mission
- Positioning
- Audience
- Personality
- Voice rules
- Tagline options
- Logo usage
- Color palette
- Typography system
- UI examples
- Do and do not examples

### Ideal size

10 to 20 pages is enough for a first version.

The goal is clarity and consistency, not volume.

---

## 12. Test and refine

Show the first version to 5 to 10 people who resemble your target users.

Ask only a few focused questions:

- What kind of app does this feel like?
- Who does this feel made for?
- How does it make you feel?
- Does it feel trustworthy?
- Does it feel motivating or stressful?

If their answers do not match your intent, adjust the brand.

---

## Recommended 10-day workflow

### Day 1

Write the brand brief and positioning.

### Day 2

Define personality and verbal identity.

### Day 3

Build three moodboards.

### Day 4

Choose one visual direction.

### Day 5

Define colors, typography, and basic UI style.

### Day 6

Explore the wordmark and app icon.

### Day 7

Design five key product screens.

### Day 8

Turn the work into a mini design system.

### Day 9

Create the brand guide.

### Day 10

Get feedback and refine.

---

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Starting with the logo
- Choosing colors before defining the emotional direction
- Copying another app's style too closely
- Making branding that looks good in a presentation but weak in-product
- Using too many fonts, colors, or ideas
- Making the brand feel like generic AI tech

---

## Final deliverables checklist

By the end, you should have:

- Brand brief
- Positioning statement
- Personality traits
- Voice guide
- Moodboard
- Color palette
- Typography pair
- Logo or wordmark
- Icon style
- Five key product screens
- Mini brand guide

---

## Best tools for the job

- Figma for most of the work
- Pinterest or Are.na for references
- Notion or Google Docs for strategy writing
- Adobe Fonts or Google Fonts for typography testing

Use color tools only for exploration, not for final brand decisions.

## Recommendation for Momentum

Aim for this balance:

- Premium
- Warm
- Focused
- Structured
- Encouraging

Avoid this territory:

- Generic startup SaaS
- Cold productivity minimalism
- Loud self-improvement hype
- Sci-fi AI branding

The brand should feel like serious progress with emotional support.
